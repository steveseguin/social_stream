Keep an eye on your name

Quick Call

Three names. Fresh four-character codes. Be ready when it is your turn.

Try the preview →

How to play

Type !quick to join. When called, type your own code within 30 seconds. Correct replies rotate back into the queue; missed turns require joining again. The overlay ranks the five fastest personal-best times. Codes ignore capitalization.

Transparent OBS background. Refreshing the browser source starts fresh. Scores stay only in this page. No bot messages, points, or purchases. Times include stream and chat delay, so this is a casual competition. Random codes discourage prepared answers; they cannot prevent automated reading.

Made for OBS layers

Play over your stream

Firefly Catch and Comet Rally leave the surrounding page transparent; Maze Raid fills its source. Start with a 1280 × 800 browser source. Preview pages show a checkerboard to make the transparent areas visible.

Three new ways to play

Firefly Catch: type !catch to start, then catch when the firefly enters the ring. One attempt per viewer per flight; a centered catch earns 3 points, other catches in the ring earn 1. Six flights, then a winner.

Comet Rally: type !boost to join an automatically assigned team. Each viewer can boost once every 3 seconds. First to 20 boosts wins, or the leader after 60 seconds.

Maze Raid: a faster auto-roaming explorer navigates textured brick-and-metal corridors with SSN wall signs. Ordinary chat spawns one avatar enemy per chatter, up to 40 per round. Raiders spawn 3 to 8 corridor steps away, chase and attack; the final hit wins. Failed or missing avatar images show initials. The maze fills its OBS browser source; resize the source to fit your scene.

All three restart after an 8-second result screen. No points are spent and no bot messages are sent.

No commands needed

Games that run on hype

Six transparent overlays that react to how excited chat is, not to what it types. Each one measures chat against what is normal for your channel, so a five-person chat and a five-thousand-person chat both get a fair game. Donations, subs, follows and raids count as bigger jolts.

Six ways to spend the excitement

Chat Quake: a live trace along the bottom of the stream. A sudden rush of chat is flagged as a quake, with a magnitude and the word chat was shouting, and the biggest one is kept as the record. Add &style=paper for a paper chart in the corner, or add &theme=night to darken the chart. Inspired by Matthew Busel's ChatQuake.

Quake City: a small skyline along the bottom. Excited chat cracks and topples the buildings one at a time. Flatten it and it is rebuilt a level taller and tougher, so the next knockdown takes more chat.

Hype Flight: chat keeps a little bird in the air through wide, easy gaps. More chat flies higher, the bird ducks under clouds by itself, and only a lull makes it miss.

Chat Volcano: chat builds pressure until it erupts, and the eruption lasts as long as the hype does. The mountain grows after each one.

Hype Rocket: chat fuels a rocket up the side of the screen, from the Cloud Deck to the Moon and beyond. Each destination is farther than the last.

Chat Campfire: every message is a log on the fire. A chat keeping its usual pace holds a steady campfire, excitement builds a bonfire, and silence lets it die down to embers.

Options: &sensitivity=low või high, &scale=1.5, &onlytype=twitch,youtube, &hidetype=kick, &hud=0 to hide the text, &shake=0 for no shaking, &color=ff8800, ja &align=left, center või right to move the volcano, rocket or campfire. The seismograph also takes &window=120 for the seconds shown and &bg=0 to drop its panel.

Built for the stream, not the page

Portrait games, team towers and show-time scenes

Seven games drawn to be watched at stream size. They show no titles and no instructions: only the game, the numbers that matter, and the one thing to type.

For portrait streams (9:16)

Peg Drop: type !drop to drop your ball from the moving chute. The pegs decide where it lands, and the gold slot pays triple. Chat Blocks: a falling-block puzzle the whole chat steers. Type a column number, 1 juurde 8, to vote where the block lands, or spin to turn it. Sky Drop: type !jump to parachute out of the airship, then left ja right to steer against the wind onto the target. In a 16:9 scene these three keep their portrait shape; add &align=left või right to move them.

Two teams, clear middle

Tower Duel: every chatter is put on one of two teams automatically. Each message drops a crate from that team's crane, and the first tower to reach the flag wins. In a 16:9 scene the towers use only the sides, so a 9:16 video fits between them untouched.

Before, during a break, and after the show

Front Row: a starting-soon countdown. The first message from each chatter drops them into a seat, and the crowd celebrates at zero. Keep It Up: for breaks. Every message bumps the balloon; chat tries to beat its longest rally. Encore: for the end of the show. Every chatter launches a firework, and a busy chat sets off a finale.

Options: &scale=0.8, &onlytype=twitch,youtube, &hidetype=kick. Peg Drop &round=180 (seconds, 0 for endless). Chat Blocks &turn=8 (seconds to vote). Sky Drop &round=75. Tower Duel &goal=12 (crates to win), &gap=0.3 (share of the width left clear), &panel=0 (no backing), &command=!build (only that command drops). Front Row &minutes=5 või &until=19:30. Keep It Up &minutes=5 to show a break timer. Encore &crowd=8 (chatters needed for the finale).

Faces first

Races, a boss, a quiz, and a crowd of your chatters

Eleven more pages built around your chatters' own pictures and names. As before: no titles, no instructions, only the game and the one thing to type.

Play

Marble Race: type !play and your marble, wearing your picture, races everyone else down an obstacle course that is different every time. Built for portrait. Cliff Climb: every message is one hop up the cliff, and the first to the flag wins. It uses only the two sides of a 16:9 scene, so a 9:16 video fits in the middle. Stream Boss: every message chips at the boss. Tips, subs and raids hit hard, members hit half again as hard, and whoever lands the final blow becomes the next boss, with their own picture in the frame. The boss heals a little whenever they chat. Trivia: a quiz show. Type a, b, c või d; faster correct answers score more. Ten question packs are built in, or load your own. Stream Bingo: one bingo card for the whole stream, with a picture in every square. When that thing happens, chat types the square's number; enough callers stamp it, and a full line is a bingo.

Always on

Chat Crowd: everyone who chats becomes a little character with their own picture, strolling along the bottom of the stream. They hop when their owner talks, show the first emoji from the message, and obey !jump, !wave, !dance, !hug ja !duel (add a name to pick who).

Before, during a break, and after the show

Roll Call: a starting-soon countdown where everyone who says hello lands on a growing wall of faces. Chat Aquarium: a calm break screen where every chatter is a fish, and a tip drops treasure for the whole tank. Group Photo: an end-of-show class photo. Everyone who says goodbye walks into frame, the camera flashes, and the picture drops out as a print. Winners' Podium: the stream's top players across every game. The games on this page award stars (5, 3 and 2 for the top three of a round, 1 for taking part); the podium adds them up. Chatters type !stars to see their own total. Stars are kept in the browser source's own storage, per session, and start again after twelve quiet hours.

One source, many games

Signal Lock

1?4?

Chat has 90 seconds to crack four digits. Every guess gives everyone a clue. Great for puzzle fans, study breaks, gaming lobbies, and smaller communities.

How to play

Type !code 1234. Digits range from 1 to 6, and can repeat. “Exact” means the correct digit and position. The other count shows correct digits in different positions.

The first valid guess starts the clock. Five seconds between each viewer’s guesses; identical guesses are ignored.

Two teams / One rope

Tug of War

Two friendly monsters pull for Team Coral and Team Mint. Correct answers from your teammates move the rope toward your side.

Try the preview →

How to play

Type !join to get assigned to the smaller team (random when tied). A match starts once both sides have a player. Teams stay fixed for that match; late joiners are welcome.

A word or simple arithmetic question appears every 12 seconds. Type the word or answer directly in chat, or use !answer followed by your answer. Each player can earn one tug per prompt; repeated answers do not add more tugs.

A lead of 20 tugs wins. Otherwise, the side ahead after three minutes wins; an even rope is a draw. After the result screen, viewers join fresh teams for the next match. No loyalty points are spent.

Protect the ship through six meteor waves. Match shields to the incoming colors.

Try the preview →

How to play

Put it on stream

  1. In SSN, open Interactive Games → Select Game.
  2. Choose one of the games above and add its session link as an OBS Browser Source. Start at 1280 × 900.
  3. Keep SSN and your chat sources running. Show the on-screen command and let chat start the round.

Rounds advance automatically. To restart a game, refresh its browser source. Add &transparent for a transparent page background, or &chroma for green. Add &demo only for an isolated preview.

These games use no loyalty points, purchases, external media, or viewer login page. They only read the chosen SSN chat session; they do not send commands back to chat. The same person on two platforms counts as two players. Up to 1,000 distinct players per round.

For Rhythm Pulse, set Volume in SSN's Game Options before copying the link. You can also add &volume=0 through 100; 0 mutes audio and the default is 50.

Host a phrase game

In SSN, open Interactive Games → Phrase Guess host tool. Enter your phrases and select Start Game. Keep this page private; use Dock to show prompts and results to viewers. Try the host preview.
